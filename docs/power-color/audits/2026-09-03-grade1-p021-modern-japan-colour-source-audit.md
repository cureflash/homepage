## Accepted corroborating boundary

### 1. Meiji-period chemical dyes and Yuzen development
Kyoto National Museum, `友禅染裂本帳`:
- The object is classified as Meiji-period Japanese textile material.
- The museum states that application of chemical dyes to Yuzen developed in the Meiji period.
- In Meiji 8 (1875), Kyoto's `舎密局` opened a dye workshop (`染殿`) and began practical instruction in dyeing with chemical dyes.
- The museum describes subsequent development using chemical dyes in colour insertion/gradation and coloured paste for `写友禅`, while retaining established Yuzen processes such as resist-paste outlines and colour insertion.

Source: https://knmdb.kyohaku.go.jp/5758.html

### 2. Imported aniline as a dating clue in late-Edo/Meiji multicolour printing
Agency for Cultural Affairs, `天神・随身図` on Cultural Heritage Online:
- The work is catalogued as a Meiji-period multicolour woodblock print.
- Its commentary states that imported chemical dye `アニリン（洋紅）` had entered Japan from the Man'en era (1860–1861) onward.
- The presence of that dye is used by the museum/catalogue commentary as evidence supporting a Meiji-period date for the print.

Source: https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/441086

### 3. Early-Shōwa colour-reference publishing by Wada Sanzō
National Diet Library catalogue records establish:
- `色名総鑑`, edited by 和田三造, published in 1931 (Shōwa 6).
- `配色総鑑`, edited by 和田三造, with editions/volumes published in 1933–1935; NDL records classify the work under colour / colour coordination.

Sources:
- https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000754647
- https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000001085468

These bibliographic facts are accepted only as modern-Japan colour-reference history. This audit does not infer exact colour chips, reconstructed appearance, individual colour-name membership, or the unpublished AFT p.021 selection of Wada facts.

## Authoring-safe fact families

Text-only questions may be built from these deterministic relations:
1. Meiji-period Yuzen ↔ development of chemical-dye application.
2. 1875 Kyoto `舎密局` ↔ opening of `染殿` / practical chemical-dye instruction.
3. Chemical dyes ↔ colour insertion/gradation and coloured-paste `写友禅` developments described by Kyoto National Museum.
4. Imported aniline (`洋紅`) ↔ present from the Man'en era onward and used as dating evidence for a Meiji multicolour print.
5. 和田三造 ↔ editor of `色名総鑑` (1931).
6. 和田三造 ↔ editor of `配色総鑑` (1930s; NDL catalogued colour/coordination publication).
