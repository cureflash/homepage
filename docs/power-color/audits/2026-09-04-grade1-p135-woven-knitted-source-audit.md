# Grade 1 p.135 織物と編物 — source audit

Classification: `SECONDARY_CORROBORATION_REQUIRED`

## Official scope
The current AFT Grade 1 official table of contents places `織物と編物` at p.135 under fashion `素材の知識`. AFT public material does not expose the detailed textbook treatment.

## Accepted corroboration
QTEC, a Japanese textile quality-testing foundation, explicitly defines woven fabric as fabric constructed by interlacing warp and weft yarns, generally at right angles. It identifies plain, twill and satin as the three basic woven structures. QTEC separately defines knitted fabric as fabric formed by connecting yarn loops, and explains that weft knitting forms loops continuously in the horizontal direction while warp knitting forms them continuously in the vertical direction.

These facts provide a narrow, deterministic, nonvisual structure boundary directly aligned to the official `織物と編物` heading. They do not establish unpublished AFT-specific performance comparisons, color-development effects, recommended applications, diagrams, or appearance judgments.

## Rejected routes
- Do not infer color development, gloss, saturation, perceived texture or other visual properties from weave/knit structure.
- Do not reconstruct AFT diagrams or geometry from QTEC illustrations.
- Do not treat every QTEC detail as Grade 1 scope; author only the basic structural relations needed to distinguish woven and knitted construction.

## Authoring boundary
Text-only questions may test: woven = warp/weft interlacing; the three basic woven structures are plain/twill/satin; knitted = connected yarn loops; weft knitting proceeds with loops in the horizontal direction while warp knitting proceeds in the vertical direction. Detailed physical/visual properties remain out of scope.
