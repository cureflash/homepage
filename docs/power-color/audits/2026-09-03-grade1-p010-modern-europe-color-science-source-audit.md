# Grade 1 p.010 `近代ヨーロッパと色彩学` source audit

Status: `SECONDARY_CORROBORATION_REQUIRED`

## Official boundary
The current AFT Grade 1 official textbook table of contents places `西洋の色彩と文化 / 近代ヨーロッパと色彩学` at p.010, after p.007 `中・近世のヨーロッパの色彩` and before p.014 `日本の古代の色`. AFT public material therefore authorizes the topic and order, but does not publicly expose the p.010 body text or an exhaustive list of its facts.

## Resolved narrow facts
The following nonvisual historical/color-science facts are independently auditable from institutional and archival authority and may be used only within the stated boundary:

1. The Royal Society archives document Isaac Newton's prism experiments and papers on the theory of light and colours in the 1670s; Royal Society records also state that this work was subsequently incorporated into Newton's `Opticks` (1704). This authorizes questions about the documented Newton/prism/light-and-colour historical relation, not reconstructed spectra or unstated experimental geometry.
2. Royal Society archival records identify Newton's 1672 prism experiment as part of his published discussion of light and colour and preserve later 1675/1676 theory-of-light-and-colours material. This authorizes chronology/association questions limited to those records.
3. Klassik Stiftung Weimar documents Goethe's `Farbenlehre` / `Zur Farbenlehre` as published in 1810 and preserves the near-complete apparatus and materials connected to his principal work on colour, including prisms and experimental materials.
4. Klassik Stiftung Weimar states that Goethe investigated physiologically, physically and chemically produced colours as well as their psychological impressions, with human sensory perception central to his studies. This authorizes only that institutional description; do not turn it into a claim that Goethe's physical theory is scientifically accepted today.
5. A Royal Society collection record for an 1817 work explicitly describes prism phenomena discussed in relation to Newton's and Goethe's theories of colour. This supports the narrow historical fact that the two colour theories were explicitly compared/debated in early nineteenth-century European colour-science literature.

## Not authorized
Do not infer or author from this audit:
- an exhaustive list of p.010 AFT facts or persons;
- that every externally corroborated Newton/Goethe fact appears verbatim in the AFT textbook;
- reconstructed prism geometry, spectral widths, wavelengths or numeric optical values not explicitly sourced;
- RGB/HEX/display values or monitor-facing colour questions;
- answers based on modern images of prisms, spectra, plates or colour diagrams;
- a universal judgment that Goethe's physical colour theory is scientifically correct; Klassik Stiftung itself notes that some conclusions are disputed or untenable from a modern scientific standpoint;
- Young–Helmholtz, Maxwell, Chevreul or other later figures unless separately source-audited and aligned to p.010 rather than later AFT chapters.

## Decision
Do not block the entire p.010 topic. AFT confirms the heading/order; Royal Society archival authority resolves a narrow Newton/prism/light-and-colour historical boundary, while Klassik Stiftung Weimar resolves a narrow Goethe/1810/perception-and-experiment boundary. Authoring may proceed only from those explicit relations. Additional p.010 facts require separate auditable source checks.
