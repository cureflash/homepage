## Resolved narrow facts
The following nonvisual facts are independently auditable from museum/scientific authority and are safe as narrow historical-color knowledge, without asserting that they exhaust p.006:

1. Egyptian blue was a synthetic pigment produced by heating a mixture including sand, lime, an alkali, and a copper compound; the product could be ground for pigment use. Authority: Metropolitan Museum of Art, `Lump of Blue Paste`.
2. Egyptian faience has a siliceous body and brightly colored glaze; copper creates its characteristic turquoise-blue coloration. Authority: Metropolitan Museum of Art, `Egyptian Faience: Technology and Production`.
3. Ancient Egyptian writing/painting materials include carbon-based black ink and red iron-oxide/red-ocher ink; Egyptian blue is documented as a copper-silicate synthetic pigment. Authority: Metropolitan Museum of Art, `Papyrus-Making in Egypt`.
4. Ancient Greek monumental sculpture was polychrome rather than necessarily bare-white marble: the British Museum documents Egyptian-blue traces on Parthenon sculptures detected by visible-induced infrared luminescence. Authority: British Museum, `Paint and the Parthenon`.
