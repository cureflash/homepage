# Grade 2 camaieu / faux-camaieu source audit

Checked: 2026-09-01

## Scope authority

The current Grade 2 official-text scope inventory places camaieu / faux-camaieu after the already resolved tonal scheme. The TOC is scope authority only; it does not by itself authorize detailed answer rules.

## Auditable explicit definitions

### Plus Colors — supervised by a Color Certification Level 1 coordinator

Source: https://pluscolors.net/color_knowledge/camaieu/

The current article, updated 2024-12-21 and explicitly referencing the 2020 revised official Color Certification text, states:
- camaieu appears almost monochromatic because hue and tone are both very close;
- representative PCCS handling is same/similar tone with same/adjacent hue;
- faux-camaieu has slightly more variation than camaieu;
- faux-camaieu uses same/similar tone with similar hue.

### ONCA — professional design-company explanation

Source: https://onca.co.jp/column/design/p8291/

The explicit text states:
- camaieu is essentially a single-hue scheme with extremely small lightness and chroma differences;
- faux-camaieu uses near hues with minute lightness/chroma differences and is a slightly hue-shifted version of camaieu.

## Authorized intersection

Power Color may encode only the common nonvisual propositions below:
1. Camaieu is an almost-monochromatic scheme in which hue/tone-related differences are kept extremely small.
2. Faux-camaieu remains a close/near-color scheme but introduces slightly more variation than camaieu, especially by shifting hue while keeping lightness/chroma differences small.
3. Both schemes rely on similarity/commonality rather than strong contrast.

Do not encode an exact numeric PCCS hue-difference boundary from this audit. Plus Colors supplies a more detailed same/adjacent-vs-similar classification, but ONCA does not publish the equivalent numeric PCCS boundary. Do not infer RGB/HEX, monitor values, sample-image appearance, or unstated tone-map geometry.
