# Grade 1 p.152 資料・光と色（1） — 色の知覚 — Source Audit

## Classification
`SOURCE_RESOLVED`

## Official scope
AFT current official Grade 1 textbook table of contents places `資料・光と色（1） — 色の知覚` at p.152. AFT's current official Grade 1 first-stage sample question directly tests nonvisual color-perception facts concerning retinal illuminance, hue change, the Bezold–Brücke phenomenon, invariant wavelengths, and examples of invariant wavelengths. This resolves a narrow current-testable slice without reconstructing unpublished textbook wording.

## Accepted authority
1. AFT current official Grade 1 textbook table of contents: https://www.aft.or.jp/images/text_of-1st-grade_mokuji.pdf
2. AFT current official Grade 1 first-stage sample question and published answer key: https://www.aft.or.jp/images/exam1.pdf and https://www.aft.or.jp/pages/feature/level

The official sample states that even at the same wavelength, changing light intensity/retinal illuminance can change perceived hue; identifies this as the Bezold–Brücke phenomenon; calls wavelengths whose perceived hue changes little with intensity `不変波長`; and, through the official answer key, identifies 474 nm and 571 nm among the examples.

## Authoring boundary
Author only text-only questions testing:
- changing retinal illuminance can change perceived hue even when wavelength is unchanged;
- this hue-change phenomenon is the Bezold–Brücke phenomenon;
- wavelengths whose perceived hue changes little as intensity changes are called invariant wavelengths (`不変波長`);
- the official current sample identifies 474 nm and 571 nm as examples of invariant wavelengths.

Do not infer:
- any unpublished AFT diagram geometry or curve shape;
- additional invariant wavelengths not explicitly resolved by the official sample/answer key;
- RGB/HEX/display values, monitor-facing color appearance, or color judgments from the printed sample image;
- an exhaustive p.152 textbook rule set.

## Decision
The p.152 narrow slice above is directly resolved by current AFT official first-stage sample authority. Classification is `SOURCE_RESOLVED`; a four-record nonvisual authoring batch is permitted.
