## Corroborating authority

### Library of Congress — Rood original work metadata

Library of Congress digitizes Ogden N. Rood's `Modern chromatics, with applications to art and industry`, published in New York by D. Appleton and Company in 1879. The catalogue subjects include color, color in art, and painting, and the public-domain digitization provides the original work as an auditable primary source.

- https://www.loc.gov/item/11032924/

### Metropolitan Museum of Art — optical mixture relation

The Met's discussion of Seurat and Rood quotes `Modern Chromatics` on placing small dots of two colours close together and allowing them to be blended by the eye at a proper viewing distance. The Met separately explains Neo-Impressionist `mélange optique` as separate touches of pigment producing colour interaction in the observer's eye rather than conventional palette mixing.

- https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/seurat
- https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/georges-seurat-1859-1891-and-neo-impressionism

### Inter-Society Color Council — concise Rood theory summary

ISCC identifies Rood as an American physicist and summarizes `Modern Chromatics` as dividing colour into three constants: purity, luminosity, and hue. It also states that Rood proposed small dots or lines of different colours blending into a new colour when viewed from a distance, and that paired complementary colours could enhance the presence of a painting.

- https://www.iscc.org/US-Color-Researchers

## Authoring-safe fact families

A small text-only p.027 batch may use only these corroborated deterministic facts:
1. Ogden N. Rood's `Modern Chromatics, with Applications to Art and Industry` was published in 1879.
2. Rood distinguishes three colour constants as purity, luminosity, and hue.
3. Rood's optical-mixture relation uses small separated dots or lines of different colours that blend perceptually when viewed from a suitable distance; this is not ordinary pigment premixing on the palette.
4. Rood associated paired complementary colours with increased chromatic presence/brilliance in painting.
5. The optical-mixture relation influenced Neo-Impressionist practice, but this historical influence must be stated only at the level explicitly supported by museum/ISCC sources.
