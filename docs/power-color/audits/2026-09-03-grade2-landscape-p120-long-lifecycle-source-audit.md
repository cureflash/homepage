## Official Grade 2 alignment

The current Color Certification Association Grade 2 official text table of contents places p.120 `ライフサイクルの長い色彩` under `景観色彩における色彩の基礎知識`, immediately after `人工要素と自然要素の色彩が共存`.

The public AFT material fixes scope/order but does not expose enough textbook prose for detailed authoring. The accepted boundary below therefore uses only propositions explicitly stated by auditable corroborating sources.

## Auditable rule sources

Accepted corroboration:
- `color_prism_landscape_approach_2026` — Color Prism, `景観色彩の考え方`, current public Grade-2-oriented explanatory page rechecked 2026-09-03.
- `itami_public_facility_landscape_guideline_2022` — Itami City, `伊丹市公共施設景観指針`, current public page rechecked 2026-09-03.

Color Prism explicitly states that buildings and bridges are artificial elements that remain in place for long periods and therefore constitute long-lifecycle color. It further states that their color planning should not prioritize fashion, novelty or individual self-assertion over harmony with surroundings. The same source notes that such artificial elements can occupy large visual areas and that outdoor materials/colors are exposed to sunlight and weather, making durability/weather resistance relevant.

Itami City independently states that public facilities remain in existence for long periods and are seen by unspecified large numbers of people, supporting the long-duration/public-landscape premise without relying on image appearance.

## Accepted micro-skill boundary

Authorize `pc2.landscape.long_lifecycle_color` only for deterministic text questions about these explicit propositions:
1. buildings and bridges are artificial landscape elements that can remain in place for long periods;
2. because their colors have long lifecycles, planning should prioritize harmony with surroundings rather than fashion, novelty or individual self-assertion;
3. artificial landscape elements may occupy large visual areas, so choosing their colors as casually as small consumer goods is not supported by the accepted source;
4. outdoor artificial elements are exposed to sunlight and weather, so durability/weather resistance is a relevant planning consideration;
5. the long-duration/public-landscape premise is reinforced by the public-sector observation that public facilities remain for long periods and are seen by many people.

## Exclusions

This audit does not authorize:
- any RGB/HEX/display value, monitor-facing swatch or image-derived answer;
- any specific hue, tone, Munsell value/chroma/notation or named color recommendation;
- any numerical durability period or repaint cycle;
- any claim that one material or coating is universally required;
- any geometry, area ratio or photograph-derived judgment;
- any universal ban on all distinctive colors; the accepted rule is contextual priority for surrounding harmony, not a numeric or categorical prohibition.

The source contract is conceptual and relational only.
