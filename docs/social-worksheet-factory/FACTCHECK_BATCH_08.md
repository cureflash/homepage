## Independent evidence review

The current MEXT high-school Geography/History commentary was reopened for 日本史探究 A(3). Generated wording was treated as untrusted. A(3) explicitly covers state formation and Kofun culture, the formation of the ritsuryo system and associated cultures, aristocratic politics, Heian-period culture, changes in local rule, and the emergence of warriors. It also asks learners to consider relations with the Chinese mainland and Korean Peninsula, relations with Sui/Tang China, changes in East Asian relations, and the growth and influence of local powers.

Specific chronology was independently cross-checked with the National Archives of Japan. Public institutional material from the National Museum of Japanese History, Nara National Museum, Kyoto's official tourism resource, and National Diet Library Search was used for selected concrete claims.

## Material corrections

- `asuka-state-formation`: replaced the generated broad claim that East Asian change simply caused political reform with the MEXT state-formation framework; retained the Taika Reform as a dated concrete example using National Archives chronology.
- `ritsuryo-state`: replaced a generic local-administration shortcut with MEXT's formal `律令体制の成立過程と諸文化の形成` framing and independently anchored 701 / 大宝律令 chronology.
- `nara-government-society`: removed `班田収授` from the title/key point because this batch did not independently establish that generated wording. The unit now uses the 710 Heijo-kyo move and independently documented ancient census registers to examine state administration.
- `tenpyo-culture-east-asia`: independently grounded the Tang-mission and Todaiji/Buddhist-culture examples in Nara National Museum material, while keeping the MEXT East-Asian-relations framework.
- `early-heian-rule`: independently anchored the 794 Heian-kyo move and rewrote the second learning point around MEXT's explicit `地方支配の変化` and ritsuryo-system transformation.
- `sekkan-politics`: aligned the unit to MEXT's `貴族政治の展開`; Fujiwara Michinaga is used only as a concrete, independently supported exemplar rather than as a substitute for the curriculum objective.
- `shoen-warrior-rise`: the generated title and causality overcommitted A(3) to `荘園・公領` and a direct warrior-growth explanation. The learner-facing title is now `地方支配の変化と武士の出現`, following MEXT. The slug is retained for compatibility; landholding specifics remain for the following medieval-transition queue.
