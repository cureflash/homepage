## Independent evidence review

The current MEXT high-school Geography/History commentary was independently reopened for 日本史探究 A(3). Generated chronology-heavy wording was not treated as evidence.

MEXT structures A(3) around two connected strands. The first covers state formation, Kofun culture, the process of establishing the ritsuryo system and the formation of ancient cultures, with particular attention to relations with Sui/Tang China and the Korean peninsula. The second covers aristocratic politics, Heian-period culture, changes in local governance and the appearance of warriors, using those developments to understand the reorganization and transformation of the ritsuryo system.

The commentary explicitly proposes examining the changing East Asian environment, rapid introduction of ritsuryo institutions, Buddhist culture, construction of Heijo-kyo and Nara-period politics, direct introduction of continental culture through envoys to Sui/Tang, policies around the move to Heian, formation of sekkan politics, changing state-to-state and private East Asian exchange, local development/security, shoen formation, changes in provincial administration/taxation and the appearance of warriors.

## Material corrections

- `asuka-state-formation`: removed the unsupported generated shortcut that simply declared the Taika Reform a curricular turning point. The unit now connects political reform and state formation to changes in East Asia and the introduction of ritsuryo institutions/Buddhism.
- `ritsuryo-state`: removed the isolated 701 / local-administrative-division memorization framing. The unit now asks why the system developed, what features the Japanese ritsuryo system had and what later influence it exerted.
- `nara-government-society`: removed unsupported generated emphasis on household-register details and the single 710 date. The unit now follows MEXT's explicit Heijo-kyo + Nara-politics treatment within the establishment of the ritsuryo system.
- `tenpyo-culture-east-asia`: aligns Tenpyo culture with envoys to Sui/Tang, direct import of continental material, Buddhism and the international character of ancient culture.
- `early-heian-rule`: replaces a 794 flashcard with comparison of policies around the Heian move, early-Heian political reform, Nara politics and changing East Asian conditions.
- `sekkan-politics`: removes the generated dependency on an unsupported Fujiwara/external-relative shortcut and instead compares the formation of sekkan politics with earlier political arrangements, as MEXT explicitly requires.
- `shoen-warrior-rise`: narrows the unit to A(3). It now treats local development/security, shoen formation, provincial administration/taxation changes and warrior emergence as evidence of ritsuryo transformation; the actual ancient-to-medieval transition remains for the following B(1) unit.
