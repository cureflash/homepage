## Independent evidence review

The current MEXT high-school Geography/History commentary was reopened from scratch for 日本史探究 B. The generated wording was treated as untrusted.

For B(1), MEXT explicitly places `貴族政治の変容と武士の政治進出` and `土地支配の変容` within the understanding of the transition from ancient to medieval Japan. The commentary further frames the period from the insei era to the establishment of warrior government, asks learners to focus on changes in the holders of power and relations with East Asia, and gives the change from ritsuryo land institutions toward warrior landholding as a learning example.

For B(2), MEXT requires learners to collect and read historical information from appropriate sources showing medieval characteristics, then consider those characteristics from multiple perspectives while respecting source characteristics and express a hypothesis. The handling notes identify records of warrior houses, court nobles, shogunates and temples/shrines, paintings, and materials based on them as possible source types.

## Material corrections

- `medieval-transition`: retained the queue theme but replaced the loose generated explanation with the formal B(1) framework. The learner-facing emphasis is now the transformation of aristocratic politics, warrior political advance, changes in land control, changes in the holders of power, and East-Asian connections.
- `landholding-transition`: removed the generated unsupported shortcut that `荘園・公領` simply formed the basis of medieval society. The learner-facing wording now follows MEXT's explicit land-control transition and its example moving from ritsuryo land institutions toward warrior landholding supporting warrior government.
- `medieval-sources-outlook`: removed the arbitrary generated list of document/scroll/archaeological-material examples and aligned the unit directly to B(2): source selection, information collection/reading, attention to source characteristics, multidimensional consideration, and hypothesis formation.
